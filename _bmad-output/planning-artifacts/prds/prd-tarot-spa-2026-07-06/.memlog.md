- (decision) Entry point: Journey-led (user-first), per Tony's confirmation
- (decision) Core mechanism (Tony's essay): draw system-pattern cards + current events -> LLM generates a reframing 'lens' (a way of seeing, not advice) -> lens applied to user's own decision context to orient before deciding
- (decision) Prompt output must: identify where the pattern shows up in the user's situation, point out what they're likely missing, challenge their framing if the question itself is wrong, give one non-obvious/counterintuitive implication, suggest better next questions
- (decision) Prompt constraints: be concrete/specific, avoid generic or widely-known advice, prefer reframing over summarizing — output is an orientation shift, not a recommendation or summary
- (decision) Today this is a fully manual process (Tony hand-picks cards, reads news, writes lens, pastes into ChatGPT with his own context) — the app automates this entire pipeline end-to-end for other users
- (decision) Lens generation is per-user, per-session — not a shared daily lens. Each user picks a draw style (existing SPREADS: single/three/decision/system from deck.js), draws that many cards, and the app generates their own personal lens from those cards + current events
- (decision) UJ-1 captured: Erica (first-gen invite friend) uses Decision spread to weigh a promotion; types full freeform context (pros/cons, want-got gaps, management hints, goals, work-life balance); hits 'Help Me Orient'; system draws cards, queries internet for 3 current events, writes essay-form systems-thinking discussion referencing a specific card (Resources) tied to her real situation; she reorients, decides, highlights a key phrase, and shares it with her husband
- (decision) Output format is essay-form prose (not bullets/list), referencing specific drawn card(s) by name/meaning woven into the discussion
- (decision) New capability implied: user can highlight/select a phrase from their orientation guide and share it (target/mechanism TBD — e.g. native share sheet, copy, or share-link)
- (decision) Growth/marketing funnel: Tony drives adoption via a LinkedIn article + a public PR-FAQ document that friends read before receiving an invite key — pre-sells the concept before signup
- (assumption) [ASSUMPTION] Context input is a single freeform text field (not structured pros/cons/goals sub-fields) — Erica 'types in that whole schmeer' as one block
- (decision) Context input box shows a contextual hint/placeholder — hint text may vary depending on the type of orientation problem, to help users know what to type
- (decision) Highlight-and-share needs no custom feature — relies entirely on native browser copy/paste/text-selection. No custom share mechanism to build. Supersedes earlier note treating this as a new capability to scope.
- (decision) Cards are 'oblique strategies' — provocations to pinch a nerve or open a thought possibility, NOT fortunes/predictions. The FR is not 'reference the specific card by name' but 'use the idea/pattern the drawn card presents to shape the discussion' — this makes the cards relevant AND keeps systems thinking as the crux of the output, since this is fundamentally an OODA-loop orientation tool. Supersedes earlier framing of literal card-name referencing.
- (decision) UJ-2 captured (a miss): Maya, a casual non-systems-thinker, weighs buying a car vs continuing to Uber. She types rich, concrete, sensory/nostalgic detail. Draw yields Major Arcana 'The Compression'; the LLM output stays abstract ('What's the minimum viable complexity?') without weaving in her specific concrete details the way Erica's session did. She disengages (makes a face, checks Instagram, wanders off), decides without using the guide, and picks the more complex/luxurious option — arguably the opposite of what the lens implied. Weeks later the phrase resurfaces in her memory unprompted.
- (decision) Quality risk surfaced by UJ-2: essay-form output must weave in the user's own stated concrete details (not just restate the card's abstract idea) to land — an ungrounded/abstract response is a real failure mode, contrasted directly against UJ-1's grounded essay
- (assumption) [ASSUMPTION] A 'miss' doesn't get a free retry within the app in v1 — redrawing consumes another unit of the daily rate limit like any other request, same as UJ-1's flow. No special 'this didn't land' recovery mechanism proposed yet.
- (decision) Delayed/residual value effect noted: even a session where the user disengages and ignores the guide can plant a phrase that resurfaces later unprompted. Hard to measure directly but relevant framing for Success Metrics — 'did they follow the advice' is the wrong metric since this is orientation, not advice.
- (decision) New feature: 'What did you decide?' follow-up reminder, sent a randomized number of days later (up to ~6) after an orientation session — light re-engagement + reflection nudge, doubles as a qualitative feedback loop on whether the guide influenced the outcome
- (decision) Follow-up nudge includes a results survey (not just a reflective email) — email links to a lightweight survey capturing what she actually decided; survey responses feed the PRD's Success Metrics directly (e.g. did the eventual decision align with the orientation, did she act at all)
