Your Orientation Guide

The pattern in your situation isn't really about whether you want the title. It's sitting one level down, in Bob and Jayden — two people whose skills have been sitting on a resume instead of getting used daily for months now. That's the Resources card showing up literally: you already have misplaced resources on your team, and you've been treating that as background noise instead of the actual signal.

What you're missing is that you've been running this as a personal cost-benefit ledger — more scope, more pay, less time — when the more useful ledger is the one sitting in front of you already: who on your team is outgrowing their seat, and how long you're willing to let that keep happening whether or not you take this job. The VP title is almost beside the point. The question underneath it is a stewardship question, not a promotion question.

So the frame worth challenging is "do I want to be VP." The better version is closer to: whose day-to-day already doesn't fit the resources they're bringing to it, and what's actually stopping you from doing something about that today, title or no title. That reframes the decision from ambition versus lifestyle into something else entirely — you're not choosing whether to want more, you're choosing whether to keep sitting on resources you already know are misallocated.

Here's the counterintuitive part: the job might matter less for what you'd gain than for what it would finally let you stop tolerating. You're making four-star lemonade because you've got the best lemons on the continent — the ingredients for a good outcome are already on your team, unused, and the title is mostly a permission slip to go use them.

Questions worth asking instead of "should I take it": What would Bob's and Jayden's next six months look like if nothing about your role changes? What's the actual cost of leaving misplaced resources in place another quarter? And if you took this job purely to fix that one thing — not for the scope, not for the pay — would it still be worth it?
